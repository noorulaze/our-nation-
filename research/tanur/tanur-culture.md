## 1. Classical Arts & The "Vettathu Sampradayam" in Kathakali

### 1.1 The Royal Innovation of Vettathu Raja
- In the annals of Kerala’s classical theatre, the kingdom of **Vettathunadu (Tanur)** holds an immortal place through the development of the **Vettathu Sampradayam (വെട്ടത്തു സമ്പ്രദായം)** in Kathakali.
- During the 17th century, the reigning Vettathu Raja was an ardent patron of Ramanattam and Kathakali. Discontented with the primitive performance mechanics of early forms, he instituted profound stylistic, musical, and sartorial reforms:
  1. **Addition of the Chenda:** The Vettathu Raja was the first to introduce the powerful, resonant **Chenda** drum into Kathakali, joining the Maddalam to heighten dramatic and heroic tension.
  2. **Vocal Revolution:** He separated the actors from singing duties, introducing dedicated background vocalists (*Ponnani* and *Singidi* singers with Ilathalam and Chengila cymbals), allowing the actors to focus entirely on Mudras (hand gestures) and Bhavas (facial expressions).
  3. **Costume & Make-up Refinements:** He introduced standardized crowns (*Kireedam*), ornate headgear, and improved facial makeup (*Chutti*), transforming Kathakali into the visual spectacle recognized worldwide today.
- This historical reform cemented Tanur's legacy as a foundational cradle of Kerala's classical performing arts.

## 2. Mappila Folk Arts & Traditions

### 2.1 Mappila Pattu (മാപ്പിളപ്പാട്ട്)
- Tanur has nurtured generations of Mappila poets, singers, and folklorists.
- Traditional Mappila songs—characterized by Arabi-Malayalam linguistic synthesis, rhythmic meters (*Ishalukal*), and heroic, romantic, or devotional themes—are deeply ingrained in the coastal lifestyle.
- Songs celebrating the sea, seafaring courage, historical struggles against colonial intruders, and devotional tributes (*Madh'h Pattu*) are sung during community weddings (*Kalyanam*), circumcision ceremonies (*Margakkalyanam*), and boat launchings.

### 2.2 Duffmuttu & Arabana Muttu (ദഫ്മുട്ട് & അറബനമുട്ട്)
- **Duffmuttu:** A rhythmic group folk art performed by men playing the *Duff* (a circular wooden frame drum covered with goat hide). Performers move in circular, coordinated choreographic steps, striking the drum and singing Arabic or Arabi-Malayalam verses.
- **Arabana Muttu:** A more intricate and rigorous frame-drum martial dance form, frequently showcased during cultural gatherings and Uroos festivals in Tanur.

### 2.3 Kolkkali (കോൽക്കളി)
- A vibrant stick-dance performed by both Hindu and Muslim communities in the Malabar coastal belt.
- In coastal Tanur, traditional Muslim Kolkkali troupes perform with distinctive rhythmic pacing, starting slowly and accelerating to breathtaking crescendos.

## 3. Temple Festivals & Sacred Folk Traditions

### 3.1 Shobhaparambu Kalankari Maholsavam & Thalappoli
- **Period:** Annually in late December (Malayalam month of Dhanu, typically December 27–29).
- **Venue:** Shobhaparambu Sree Kurumba Bhagavathi Temple.
- **Key Ritual Components:**
  - **Kaalavarav (കാളവരവ്):** Giant, gorgeously decorated artificial bull and ox effigies crafted with straw, wood, and colorful silk are carried through town in festive processions sponsored by neighboring *desams*.
  - **Thalappoli (താലപ്പൊലി):** Hundreds of female devotees dressed in traditional Kasavu sarees carry brass plates containing glowing oil lamps, paddy, flowers, and coconut offerings in ceremonial circumambulation.
  - **Singari Melam & Kavadiyattam:** Energetic percussion ensembles accompanied by devotional Kavadi dancers.
  - **Puthan Kalam Nivedyam:** Traditional ritual offering cooked with newly harvested unpolished rice in fresh terracotta pots (*puthan kalam*), offered to appease the sub-deity *Dandan*.
  - **Guruthi Pooja:** Midnight esoteric ritual conducted at the sanctum to invoke divine protective blessings over the village.

### 3.2 Keraladeshapuram Temple Aarattu & Pooram
- **Period:** Vrischikam (November–December).
- **Venue:** Keraladeshapuram Sree Krishna Temple.
- **Celebration Highlights:**
  - Traditional Panchavadyam and Pandi Melam performed by renowned Malabar percussionists.
  - Ceremonial ritual bath of the deity (*Aarattu*) conducted in sacred waters with caparisoned elephants, Vedic recitations, and classical temple illuminations.

## 4. Coastal Gastronomy & Culinary Heritage

### 4.1 Fresh Catch & Seafood Mastery
- Tanur's cuisine is defined by its deep relationship with the Arabian Sea. Seafood is purchased directly from the harbour wharves within hours of landing.
- **Tanur Meen Curry (താനൂർ മീൻകറി):**
  - Prepared in traditional porous terracotta pots (*Meen Chatti*).
  - Uses freshly caught **Mathi (Oil Sardines)** or **Ayala (Indian Mackerel)** simmered with Kokum / Kudampuli (*Garcinia cambogia*), thick freshly pressed coconut milk, curry leaves, fenugreek, green chilies, and fiery shallot-infused coconut oil tempering.
  - Locals maintain that the curry tastes superior when aged overnight (*Pazham Kary*).
- **Meen Porichathu (Fish Fry):** Marinated in a freshly ground paste of crushed shallots, dried red chilies, turmeric, garlic, ginger, and curry leaves, pan-fried shallowly in pure unadulterated coconut oil.
- **Kallummakkaya (Green Mussels) Delicacies:** Collected from coastal rocks or brought from the estuary; prepared as *Arikadukka* (spiced rice paste stuffed in mussel shells and steamed, then deep fried) and spicy mussel dry roast (*Kallummakkaya Ulathiyathu*).

### 4.2 Malabar Staples
- **Pathiri Varieties:** Ultra-thin rice rotis (*Ari Pathiri*), stuffed fish steamed pastry (*Meen Pathiri*), layered sweet-savory pie (*Chatti Pathiri*), and coin pathiri (*Noolputtu / Idiyappam*).
- **Neychoru & Coastal Biryani:** Fragrant Jeerakasala (Kaima) rice gently cooked in pure ghee, caramelized onions, cloves, cinnamon, and whole spices, paired with coastal spicy mutton or chicken roast.
- **Coastal Evening Snacks:** Sold along the beach stalls and tea shops:
  - *Unnakaya* (boiled plantain mashed, filled with grated coconut, cashew, and raisins, fried golden).
  - *Pazham Nirachathu* (stuffed ripe banana).
  - *Mutta Mala* & *Mutta Surka* (egg yolk strings in sugar syrup, paired with egg white cake).

## 5. Literary & Intellectual Culture

### 5.1 Travel Literature & Library Movement
- Tanur possesses an unusually rich culture of **travel literature (സഞ്ചാര സാഹിത്യം)** and public reading.
- Influenced by the pioneering travelogues of nearby Tirur and Malabar intellectuals (such as S.K. Pottekkatt and K.P. Kesava Menon), Tanur has supported dedicated travel literary clubs and community reading rooms (*Vayanasalas*).
- The **Tanur Public Library** and local study forums host periodic literary discussions, book releases, and lectures on Malabar maritime lore and oral histories.
