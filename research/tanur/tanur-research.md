## A. Tanur Overview

### 1. Identity & Essence
- **Town Name:** Tanur (Malayalam: **താനൂർ**, also transliterated historically as *Tanoor* or *Thanniyur*).
- **Location:** Coastal municipal town situated in the Tirur Taluk of Malappuram District in the northern (Malabar) region of Kerala, India.
- **Coordinates:** 10.9768° N, 75.8675° E.
- **Significance:** A historic maritime trading port with origins tracing back over a millennium. Formerly the royal capital of the **Kingdom of Vettathunadu** (*Tanur Swaroopam*).
- **Core Character:** A vibrant, living coastal community that marries rich heritage (Kerala’s first railway line, ancient Vishnu and Bhagavathi temples, centuries-old Mappila culture, and classical Kathakali reforms) with a thriving contemporary marine fishing economy, modern educational institutions, and an active civic life.

## B. Geography and Administrative Details

### 1. Administrative Status & Boundaries
- **Local Body Type:** **Tanur Municipality (താനൂർ നഗരസഭ)**.
- **Upgrade Date:** Upgraded from Grama Panchayat to Municipality on **November 1, 2015** (Kerala Piravi Day).
- **Area:** **19.49 sq. km**.
- **Wards:** **45 Electoral Divisions**.
- **District:** Malappuram.
- **Taluk:** Tirur.
- **Legislative Assembly Constituency:** Tanur (Constituency No. 44).
- **Parliamentary Constituency:** Ponnani Lok Sabha Constituency.
- **Police Jurisdiction:** Tanur Sub-Divisional Police Office (headed by DySP) and Tanur Police Station.

### 2. Physical Geography & Boundaries
- **North:** Parappanangadi Municipality and Vallikkunnu (partially demarcated by the Poorappuzha River).
- **East:** Thanalur and Ozhur Grama Panchayats.
- **South:** Tirur Municipality and Niramaruthur Grama Panchayat.
- **West:** The Arabian Sea (an unbroken sandy coastline of approximately 8.5 km).
- **Elevation:** Low-altitude coastal littoral plain averaging 3 to 7 metres above mean sea level.
- **Drainage & River System:** The **Poorappuzha River** flows along the northern edge of the municipal territory and forms a prominent estuary (*Azhimugam*) as it empties into the Arabian Sea near Ottumpuram / Thooval Theeram.

### 3. Critical Administrative Distinction
- **Tanur Municipality** is an urban local self-governing body managing its 19.49 sq. km municipal territory.
- **Tanur Block Panchayat** is an entirely separate rural administrative entity headquartered in the region that administers five neighboring rural panchayats: *Thanalur*, *Ozhur*, *Niramaruthur*, *Ponmundam*, and *Cheriyamundam*. The two must never be conflated.

## C. History and Historical Timeline

### 1. Etymological Roots
- Derived linguistically from **"Thanni-Ur" (താന്നി-ഊര്)**, signifying a human settlement surrounded by Bastard Myrobalan trees (*Terminalia bellirica*), which grew extensively along this coastal strip.
- Popular folk etymology attributes it to **"Thazhna-ur" (താഴ്ന്ന ഊര്)**, indicating a low-lying coastal land.
- Ancient maritime historians have debated links with the classical Chera port of **Tyndis**.

### 2. The Kingdom of Vettathunadu (Tanur Swaroopam)
- Served as the capital of the **Vettathiri Rajas** (Kshatriya dynasty).
- While historically a vassal of the **Zamorin of Calicut (Samoothiri)**, the rulers of Tanur frequently sought autonomy and foreign alliances.

### 3. European Encounters & Strategic Alliances
- **Portuguese Contact & St. Francis Xavier (1546):** Francis Xavier visited Tanur, engaged with local residents, and recorded observations of the sacred Keraladeshapuram Temple.
- **Royal Conversion (1549):** The King of Tanur traveled to Goa and converted to Christianity, taking the name *Dom João*, allying with Portugal to check Calicut’s dominance and facilitating Portuguese control over Chaliyam Fort.
- **French Factory ("French Chappa", 1739):** The French East India Company secured commercial trading and fish-salting concessions in Tanur, leaving the heritage place-name *French Chappa* / *Chappapadi*.

### 4. Mysorean Invasions, British Annexation & Railway
- **1766–1790:** Mysore campaigns under Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan; construction of the historic *Tipu Sultan Road*.
- **1792–1793:** Ceded to the British under the Treaty of Seringapatam; the Vettathunadu dynasty died out without natural heirs on **May 24, 1793**, escheating to the British East India Company.
- **1861 (March 12):** Kerala’s **very first railway line** was opened between Tirur and Beypore (Chaliyam), establishing **Tanur Railway Station (TA)** as one of the inaugural railway stations in Kerala history.

### 5. Freedom Struggle & 1921 Malabar Rebellion
- Tanur was a major center of Khilafat resistance in coastal Malabar.
- Rebels systematically sabotaged railway lines and telegraph wires between Tanur and Tirur to impede British military troop trains.
- British forces enforced martial law, leading to mass arrests and the subsequent detention of individuals involved in the tragic *Wagon Tragedy* of November 1921.

## D. Famous Places and Landmarks

1. **Tanur Beach & Promenade (10.9768° N, 75.8560° E):** Scenic sandy coastline, coconut groves, sunset views, and local street gastronomy.
2. **Tanur Fishing Harbour & Breakwater (10.9740° N, 75.8520° E):** Twin granite breakwaters (*Pulimuttu*), automated ice plants, and daily fish auction wharves.
3. **Keraladeshapuram Sree Krishna Temple (10.9520° N, 75.8690° E):** Located ~3 km south of Tanur. One of Kerala's oldest surviving Vishnu temples; patronized by Vettathiri kings; visited by St. Francis Xavier in 1546.
4. **Ottumpuram Beach & Thooval Theeram (10.9920° N, 75.8480° E):** Estuarine convergence of the Poorappuzha River and the Arabian Sea, featuring sandbars, mangrove vegetation, and migratory bird habitats.
5. **Shobhaparambu Sree Kurumba Bhagavathi Temple (10.9850° N, 75.8750° E):** Celebrated for the annual *Kalankari Maholsavam*, *Kaalavarav* (bull effigy pageantry), and *Thalappoli*.
6. **Islahul Uloom Arabic College (10.9780° N, 75.8690° E):** Established in 1924 by Pangil Ahmed Kutty Musliyar; historic higher Islamic learning center with manuscript archives.
7. **Tanur Town Juma Masjid (10.9760° N, 75.8640° E):** Historic merchant mosque with traditional Malabar tiered wooden architecture.
8. **Tanur Railway Station - TA (10.9772° N, 75.8670° E):** Operational since March 12, 1861 on the historic Tirur–Beypore line.
9. **Thrikkaikkattu Shiva Temple (10.9680° N, 75.8720° E):** Ancient circular sanctum dedicated to Lord Shiva.
10. **Rayirimangalam & Ganapathiyangadi Heritage Quarter (10.9810° N, 75.8710° E):** Traditional artisan enclave and Ganapathi shrine.

## E. Culture and Traditions

1. **The Vettathu Reform in Kathakali:** The Vettathu Raja in the 17th century introduced revolutionary reforms: adding the **Chenda** drum, separating singers (*Ponnani* and *Singidi*) from actors, and standardizing elaborate facial make-up (*Chutti*) and crowns (*Kireedam*).
2. **Mappila Arts:** Rich tradition of Mappila Pattu, Duffmuttu, Arabana Muttu, and Kolkkali performed at cultural gatherings and family celebrations.
3. **Temple Festivals:**
   - *Shobhaparambu Kalankari Festival:* December 27–29, featuring bull pageantry (*Kaalavarav*), Singari Melam, Kavadiyattam, *Thalappoli* with hundreds of women carrying oil lamps, and *Puthan Kalam* offerings.
   - *Keraladeshapuram Ulsavam:* Vrischikam (Nov–Dec) Aarattu with caparisoned elephants and Panchavadyam.
4. **Coastal Gastronomy:** Earthen pot fish curry (*Tanur Meen Chatti Curry* with Kudampuli and coconut oil), fried sardines and mackerels, stuffed mussels (*Arikadukka*), Pathiri varieties, and ghee rice (*Neychoru*).
5. **Travel Literature:** Vibrant culture of travel writing reading and community library discussions.

1. **Demographics (Census 2011):**
   - Municipality Total Population: **69,534**.
   - Census Town Statistical Unit: **44,973** (Males: 21,452, Females: 23,521).
   - Sex Ratio: **1,096 females per 1,000 males**.
   - Child Population (0–6 years): 6,750 (Sex ratio: 953).
   - Literacy Rate: **91.18%** (Male: 94.15%, Female: 88.55%).
2. **Social Fabric:** Harmonious multi-faith community comprising Muslim and Hindu families living in mutual cooperation.
3. **Fisherfolk Lifestyle:** Generations of traditional fishermen operating country craft (*Vallangal*) and mechanized boats, governed by customary marine knowledge.
4. **Gulf Diaspora Impact:** Strong remittance economy enabling modern home construction, educational empowerment, charitable hospitals, and retail growth.
5. **Women Empowerment:** Dynamic Kudumbashree network running micro-enterprises in hygienic dry fish production, tailoring, and catering.

## G. Fishing and Economy

1. **Marine Fisheries Volume:** Over 5,000 direct fishermen and 15,000 indirect workers. Major catches include sardines (*Mathi*), mackerel (*Ayala*), seer fish (*Neymeen*), prawns, and squids.
2. **Cold Chain & Inter-State Trade:** Active pre-dawn auctions, industrial block ice plants, and daily reefer trucks transporting fresh catch to Coimbatore, Bangalore, Madurai, Chennai, and interior Kerala.
3. **Ancillary Marine Engineering:** Fiberglass boatbuilding yards, marine engine mechanical workshops (Yamaha/Suzuki OBMs), and nylon net manufacturing.
4. **Agro-Trade & Copra:** Traditional copra drying yards, coconut oil expeller mills, coir extraction, and trade in GI-tagged **Tirur Betel Leaf**.

## H. Education and Institutions

1. **C.H. Muhammed Koya Memorial Govt. Arts and Science College, Tanur (`gctanur.ac.in`):** Established in 2013 at Puthentheru; offers BA English, BBA, BCA, BSc Electronics, BCom, Integrated MA Malayalam.
2. **Islahul Uloom Arabic College (`islahululoom.in`):** Established in October 1924 by Pangil Ahmed Kutty Musliyar; higher Islamic studies and classical Arabic linguistics.
3. **Govt. Regional Fisheries Technical Vocational HSS (RFTVHSS):** Administered by the Fisheries Department; vocational training in marine fisheries, seafood processing, and sports development.
4. **Devadhar Govt. Higher Secondary School (DGHSS):** Established 1922–1924 in adjacent Tanalur; premier centennial public school serving the Tanur region.
5. **School & Madrasa Network:** Govt. High School Tanur, Aided Mappila High Schools, Crescent English School, Al-Farook HSS, and primary Madrasas.

## L. Nature and Environment

1. **Coastal Marine Ecosystem:** 8.5 km sandy coast with littoral dunes, casuarina windbreaks, and sea turtle nesting pockets.
2. **Poorappuzha Estuary & Mangroves:** Estuarine brackish wetland hosting true mangrove species (*Rhizophora mucronata*, *Avicennia officinalis*) and associated crabs, prawns, and juvenile fish.
3. **Avian Biodiversity:** Winter migratory waterbirds at the estuary, including sandpipers, terns, gulls, egrets, and plovers.
4. **Environmental Challenges & Conservation:** Coastal erosion during heavy southwest monsoons (addressed via groynes/pulimuttu), river mouth siltation, and community-led mangrove afforestation campaigns.

## Q. Unverified Information and Research Gaps

1. **Exact Identification of Tyndis:** While several local writers and informal articles identify Tanur as the ancient port of Tyndis, academic historians remain divided, with competing claims for Kadalundi and Ponnani. *Status: Marked as debated historical hypothesis.*
2. **Pre-16th Century Written Epigraphy of Keraladeshapuram:** While physical architecture and local temple chronicles place the temple in the Kulasekhara Chera era (c. 9th–10th century), conclusive published stone inscriptions dating its exact foundation year require formal Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) epigraphical confirmation. *Status: Temple age verified as ancient/Kulasekhara era by Kerala Tourism, but precise foundation year left unstated.*
3. **Post-2011 Official Census Figures:** Due to the national postponement of the 2021 decennial census, current population projections for Tanur Municipality vary between 75,000 and 85,000. *Status: Only official 2011 Census figures (69,534 municipality / 44,973 CT) are published as authoritative facts.*
4. **Precise Extent of the 1739 French Loge:** While the local name *French Chappa* is universally attested in municipal records and local speech, the physical French colonial factory buildings were dismantled in the 18th century, and only the site memory remains. *Status: Recorded as historical heritage site.*
