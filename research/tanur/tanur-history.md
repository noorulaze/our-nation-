# Tanur (താനൂർ) — In-Depth Historical Research Dossier

**Author/Investigator:** Nammude Naadu Research & Verification Desk  
**Geographical Focus:** Tanur, Tirur Taluk, Malappuram District, Kerala  
**Date of Research:** November 2026  
**Primary Sources:** Local Self Government Department (LSGD) Kerala, Malabar Manual (William Logan), Southern Railway Archives, Jesuit Historical Archives (St. Francis Xavier letters), Census of India, Islahul Uloom Archives.

## 1. Etymology and Early Origins

### 1.1 The Name "Tanur" (താനൂർ)
- **Primary Linguistic Derivation:** Historical linguists and local tradition widely derive "Tanur" from **"Thanni-Ur" (താന്നി-ഊര്)**.
  - *Thanni (താന്നി):* Refers to the Bastard Myrobalan tree (*Terminalia bellirica*), which historically grew in abundance along this coastal tract.
  - *Ur (ഊര്):* Classical Dravidian term for a village, human settlement, or town.
  - *Evolution:* Thanni-ur → Thanniyur → Tanur.
- **Secondary Folk Derivation:** A popular local alternative claims the name stems from **"Thazhna-ur" (താഴ്ന്ന ഊര്)**, meaning a "low-lying settlement", reflecting its low sea-level coastal topography. Linguists consider the botanical origin ("Thanni-ur") significantly more robust.
- **Classical Maritime Identifications:** Some regional historians and epigraphists have suggested a possible link between Tanur and the ancient Chera port of **Tyndis**, mentioned in the Greco-Roman periplus texts and Pliny's natural history. While other historians identify Tyndis with Kadalundi (Ponnani or Pantalayani Kollam), Tanur was undeniably an active early sea-trading post with trade links to the Red Sea, Arabia, and the Mediterranean.

## 2. The Kingdom of Vettathunadu (Tanur Swaroopam)

### 2.1 Feudal Status and Dynasty
- **Name of Principality:** Known historically as **Vettathunadu** (also spelled *Bettat* or *Vettattunad*), *Prakashika*, or the **Tanur Swaroopam**.
- **Capital:** The coastal town of **Tanur** served as the seat and capital of the Vettathiri Rajas (Kshatriya dynasty).
- **Territory:** Spanned significant parts of contemporary coastal Malappuram, including Tanur, Tirur, Tanalur, Niramaruthur, Ozhur, and areas extending south towards the Bharathappuzha.
- **Dynastic Lineage:** The rulers held the dynastic title *Vettathiri*. They were known patrons of Sanskrit literature, Kathakali, and classical temple arts.
- **Relationship with the Zamorin:** The Vettathu Raja was nominally a subordinate chieftain (vassal) to the **Zamorin of Calicut (Samoothiri)**. However, the Vettathiri Rajas frequently rebelled or sought independent foreign alliances whenever the Zamorin attempted to assert centralized dominance.

## 3. Portuguese Arrival and European Contact (16th Century)

### 3.1 Diplomatic Realignment with the Portuguese
- When Vasco da Gama and subsequent Portuguese armadas arrived on the Malabar Coast (from 1498 onwards) and entered into fierce naval conflict with the Zamorin, the ruler of Tanur saw an opportunity to liberate his kingdom from Calicut's suzerainty.
- The King of Tanur opened negotiations with Portuguese governors and permitted Portuguese merchant fleets to take refuge and trade at Tanur port.

### 3.2 St. Francis Xavier’s Historic Visit (1546)
- In **1546**, **St. Francis Xavier**, the Papal Nuncio and Jesuit missionary, visited Tanur during his journey along the Malabar Coast.
- Documented in Portuguese Jesuit correspondence, Xavier met with local inhabitants and the royal household of Vettathunadu.
- Xavier also visited the ancient **Keraladeshapuram Temple** complex near Tanur, noting its architectural grandeur and religious significance to the coastal kingdom.

### 3.3 Royal Conversion and Strategic Fall-Out (1549)
- Influenced by continuous Portuguese diplomacy, military promises against the Zamorin, and Jesuit missionaries, the reigning King of Tanur traveled to Goa and was formally baptized in **1549**, adopting the Christian name **Dom João**.
- This royal conversion shook Malabar geopolitics. The Zamorin launched retaliatory expeditions against Vettathunadu.
- Under Portuguese influence, the King of Tanur assisted the Portuguese in securing access to trade routes and maintaining their strategic garrison at the nearby **Chaliyam Fort (Fortaleza de Chaliyam)**, which monitored the rivermouth just north of Tanur until the Zamorin famously besieged and destroyed it in 1571.

## 4. French Mercantile Presence: The "French Chappa" (18th Century)

### 4.1 The French East India Company at Tanur
- In the 18th century, amidst Anglo-French rivalries on the Malabar Coast, the French East India Company (headquartered in Mahé) established subsidiary commercial factories and coastal enclaves known as *loges* (lodges).
- Around December 1739, French agents secured rights to dry and salt fish, store trade goods, and hoist the French flag in a designated coastal pocket at Tanur.
- **The "French Chappa" (ഫ്രഞ്ച് ചാപ്പ):** This site evolved into a fish-curing yard and maritime depot. In coastal Kerala terminology, a *chappa* refers to a salt-fish curing and tax-stamping yard. To this day, local elders and municipal records identify this heritage pocket in Tanur as *French Chappa* or *Chappapadi*.

## 5. Mysore Invasion and the End of the Dynasty

### 5.1 Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan’s Campaigns (1766–1790)
- In the latter half of the 18th century, Mysore forces invaded Malabar.
- Tipu Sultan constructed strategic military supply routes across the coastal plain. A section of this historic road network passes through Tanur and is still recognized locally as **Tipu Sultan Road**.
- During the Mysorean military operations, the ancient Keraladeshapuram Temple faced raids; local traditions record that the chief idol was concealed in a deep step-well or transferred temporarily to safeguard it from desecration.

### 5.2 The 1792 Treaty of Seringapatam and the 1793 Lapse
- Following Tipu Sultan’s defeat in the Third Anglo-Mysore War, Malabar was formally ceded to the British East India Company under the **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)**.
- On **May 24, 1793**, the last legitimate ruler of the Vettathunadu royal line died without natural heirs or legal adoptions.
- Under the English doctrine of lapse/escheat, the entire royal estate of Vettathunadu—including the town of Tanur—escheated to the British East India Company. The territory was reorganized into the **Vettattunad Taluk**, which was later integrated into the larger **Tirur Taluk** of the Malabar District within the Madras Presidency.

## 6. Railway Modernisation: Kerala’s First Railway Line (1861)

### 6.1 The Historic Tirur–Beypore Line
- On **March 12, 1861**, the Madras Railway Company inaugurated the very **first railway line in the history of Kerala**.
- The inaugural 19-mile (approx. 30.5 km) section connected **Tirur** with the port terminal at **Beypore (Chaliyam)**.
- **Tanur Railway Station (Station Code: TA):** Tanur was one of the original intermediate stations on this maiden line.
- The arrival of the railway transformed Tanur from a purely maritime and bullock-cart trading post into an essential export corridor for dried fish, copra, coir yarn, and timber bound for Madras, Coimbatore, and Bangalore.

---

## 7. The Malabar Rebellion of 1921 & Khilafat Movement

### 7.1 Strategic Sabotage and Clashes
- During the **1921 Malabar Rebellion**, Tanur was a major center of Khilafat and anti-colonial activity along the coastal railway route.
- Understanding that the British army utilized troop trains from Podanur and Shoranur, local rebels systematically tore up railway tracks, dismantled fishplates, and cut telegraph wires between Tanur, Parappanangadi, and Tirur to prevent troop deployments.
- British forces under martial law conducted intensive searches across coastal Tanur, arresting numerous Khilafat leaders and fishermen suspected of aiding the insurrection.
- Many detainees captured in and around Tanur were among those marched to Tirur railway station, where prisoners were subsequently boarded into the fatal freight car on November 10, 1921 (the *Wagon Tragedy*).

## 8. Modern Administrative Evolution

### 8.1 Panchayati Raj Era
- **1950:** Establishment of the Tanur Village Panchayat under the Madras Village Panchayats Act.
- **1956:** Integration into the newly formed State of Kerala.
- **1964:** Reorganization of local bodies under the Kerala Panchayats Act, creating the Tanur Grama Panchayat.
- **1969:** Inclusion within the newly created **Malappuram District** (carved out of Kozhikode and Palakkad districts on June 16, 1969).

### 8.2 Upgrade to Municipality (2015)
- On **November 1, 2015** (Kerala Piravi Day), the Government of Kerala officially upgraded Tanur from a Grama Panchayat to **Tanur Municipality (താനൂർ നഗരസഭ)**.
- **Jurisdictional Extent:** 19.49 sq. km area divided into 44 (later 45) electoral wards.
- **Administrative Independence:** Tanur Municipality operates as an urban local body distinct from the **Tanur Block Panchayat**, which governs the surrounding rural panchayats of Ozhur, Ponmundam, Thanalur, Niramaruthur, and Cheriyamundam.
